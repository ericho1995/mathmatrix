# Archetypes (distilled from VCAA 2017–2022 and the 2023–2024 trials)

Each Section B question is one context with 3–6 parts that climb: read or compute → apply → explain.

Every set so far has at least one of each of the following, and new sets should keep it that way:
- a **"show that"** part, which gives the answer so that later parts don't collapse;
- a **graph to read** from the figure;
- a **sketch on the axes provided**;
- a **2-mark explanation** marked on two named ideas.

Use `coverage.mjs` for the exact stems. The lists below are the Section B contexts already used, then archetypes still unused. **Prefer unused contexts.** A used archetype is fine in a new context: use different numbers and a different storyline.

## U3 AOS1 Motion (Section B 4 questions, 23–24 marks)

**Used**
- S1: block on a table pulled by a hanging mass (a, T, why T < W); soccer ball over a wall (level launch); roller-coaster loop top (N, v_min); crash-test impulse and crumple zone.
- S2: box pulled up a ramp with friction (then released); ball thrown from a roof (quadratic time); conical pendulum (T, v); two-trolley collision with an elastic check.
- S3: car towing a trailer (third law); basketball reaching a hoop (rising or falling); ballistic pendulum; spring launcher over a hill.
- S4: lift v–t graph (tension in two phases, area = distance); package dropped from a plane (angle at impact); banked track, drawing the forces; exploding firework.
- S5: skier on a slope with friction; motorbike clearing a gap (minimum speed); vertical circle, top and bottom tension; spring bumper (impulse with rebound).

**Unused**
- Atwood machine.
- Car over a hump (Section B version).
- Bungee jumper: elastic plus gravitational energy, and the maximum extension.
- F–t graph for a collision in Section B.
- Power of a car climbing a hill at constant speed.
- Rocket or gas recoil.
- Braking distance against speed (a table, then v²).
- Seat or ride at the bottom of a circle.
- Force–extension graph area for a non-ideal spring.
- Projectile landing on a platform above or below the launch height.

**Section A staples:**
- third-law pairs and terminal velocity;
- the top of a projectile's path and flight time;
- banked, hump and swing questions;
- inelastic KE loss, recoil and Δp with rebound;
- a spring's energy or k; power; stopping distance ∝ v²; contact force between blocks.

## U3 AOS2 Fields (3 questions, 18–19 marks)

**Used**
- S1: 12-hour orbit (show r, v, weightlessness); electron between plates (E, F, v); mass spectrometer (B direction, m, no work, isotope).
- S2: Mars g and Phobos period; electron deflected crossing plates (parabola, ignore gravity); proton circle (B direction, v, T, alpha radius).
- S3: g–r graph, area → energy estimate (count squares); two point charges, field at the midpoint; velocity selector (E/B, faster ones deflect, radius).
- S4: Earth's field at the Moon → orbital speed, null point; electron gun (compare protons); rail rod (F, direction, back-EMF).
- S5: ISS (r, v, T, g, drag); Millikan oil drop (q, electrons, extra electron → a); field-model comparison (F_E/F_G for protons, monopoles).

**Unused**
- Geostationary radius from T = 24 h.
- Kepler table for moons (T² against r³ → mass of the planet; doubles as an investigation).
- Comparing g on two planets from mass and radius ratios.
- Sketching field lines (a point charge near a plate, or like/unlike pairs).
- Right-hand grip rule for a solenoid, with a compass.
- Cyclotron (period independent of speed).
- Field and force between charged plates for an alpha particle.
- Energy needed to reach orbit from a g–r graph (a variant of S3).

**Section A staples:**
- g at altitude with nR (altitude versus radius trap); T ratio ∝ r^1.5; v ∝ r^−½;
- a force scaling with charge, mass and distance; null points;
- the direction of the force on an electron (plates, magnetic);
- the magnetic force parallel to B being zero; a solenoid's field; a geostationary orbit.

## U3 AOS3 Electricity (3 questions, 23–24 marks)

**Used**
- S1: DC motor (F on a side, zero on the parallel side, couple, commutator); generator (Φ max, average EMF over a quarter turn, sketch, slip rings versus commutator); wind farm 1:20 (I, loss, drop, why not direct).
- S2: loop entering a field (ΔΦ, EMF, Lenz direction, current, EMF–t sketch through three stages); phone charger transformer (ratio, Iₚ, V_peak, DC fails, losses); farm line with step-up and step-down.
- S3: piecewise flux–time graph → EMF values and sketch; 325 V peak alternator (RMS, power, peak power, half speed); 330 kV line with a 15:1 substation.
- S4: cosine flux graph → frequency, average EMF, sketch, doubled speed; suburb 11 kV transformer (laminations, thick secondary); solar farm at 22 kV against 66 kV (factor of 9, inverter).
- S5: EMF graph → f, RMS, lamp power, commutator sketch, coil orientation, brushes; magnet falling in a copper pipe (Lenz, slit, plastic); hydro 220 kV (efficiency, half R against double V).

**Unused**
- Coil positions matched to points on an EMF graph (a diagram of four orientations).
- Thomson jumping ring (AC against DC).
- Induction cooktop, or regenerative braking.
- Back EMF in a motor (speeds up → current falls).
- A non-ideal transformer with efficiency and heating.
- Household distribution with two transformer stages and a voltage-drop table.
- DC generator output compared with a battery.

**Section A staples:**
- flux with an angle (sin versus cos trap); EMF with N turns;
- table MC for the effect of doubling the rotation (peak | frequency);
- Lenz table (pole | force); transformer ratios, currents and efficiency;
- RMS, peak and peak-to-peak; line loss I²R against the V²/R trap; loss when the voltage doubles (¼);
- commutator function; torque greatest when the plane is parallel to B.

## U4 AOS1 Light, matter and relativity (4 questions, 24–25 marks)

**Used**
- S1: 532 nm double slit (Δx, path difference to the third dark band, wave evidence, red laser); photoelectric E_k–f graph (f₀, φ, gradient = h, v, below threshold); Davisson–Germer 54 V with X-rays of the same λ; spacecraft at 0.90c to a star 12 ly away.
- S2: 400 nm stopping voltage (φ, f₀, why waves fail); 4-level atom (number of lines, longest λ, electron collision energies, photon not absorbed); electron microscope at 5 kV (p, λ, resolution, ×4 V); electron at 0.99c (γ, E_total, E_k MeV, can't reach c).
- S3: V₀–f graph for two metals (parallel lines, φ, V₀); single-photon double slit (duality); D–T fusion mass defect (MeV, reactions per second); electron versus neutron at the same λ (E_k values).
- S4: photocell I–V graph (E_k, λ, electrons per second, sketch doubled intensity); red laser (d, one slit covered, white light); muons from 15 km (γ, times, contracted distance, fraction surviving); e⁺e⁻ annihilation (E, λ, p, two photons).
- S5: sunlight (photons per m² per second, momentum, force); mercury levels and fluorescent coating; LHC protons (γ from TeV, lap times, contracted ring); electron double slit at 150 V (λ, Δx, ×4 V).

**Unused**
- Hydrogen Balmer lines from a level diagram, matched to a spectrum picture.
- Fraunhofer (absorption) lines in sunlight.
- Laser sail or solar sail momentum.
- Fission mass defect (U-235) and energy per kilogram.
- Simultaneity thought experiment (train and lightning), qualitative.
- Michelson–Morley in Section B (the expected result against the null result).
- GPS time dilation (special relativity only).
- Diffraction limit for choosing an electron or light microscope.
- Electron-diffraction ring radius against accelerating voltage (graph).
- Photoelectric graph for two light sources in the same cell.

**Section A staples:**
- fringe spacing scaling;
- table MC for the effect of intensity (E_k max | current);
- de Broglie from V (the J/eV and photon traps); energy-level λ with neighbouring-line distractors;
- dilation or contraction with the 1/γ and γ² traps; the postulates; the Michelson–Morley meaning;
- the Sun's mass loss P/c² (the P/c trap); photon momentum; single-slit diffraction; particle-model evidence;
- baseball λ; the electron:proton λ ratio at the same speed; threshold λ; photons per second.

## U4 AOS2 Investigation (1 question, 10 marks, plus 1 MC)

**Used**
- S1: current balance (F against I → B, systematic error from the wire's length).
- S2: whirling stopper (F against v² → mass, friction at the tube).
- S3: trolley down a ramp (v² against h → g, friction).
- S4: magnet dropped through a coil (EMF against v, prediction, Faraday).
- S5: LED Planck's constant (V against 1/λ → h, judging the threshold by eye).

**Unused**
- Kepler T² against r³ for Jupiter's moons → mass of Jupiter.
- Double-slit Δx against L → λ.
- Spring extension against load → k, and the energy from the area.
- Transformer V₂ against N₂.
- Photoelectric V₀ against f → h and φ.
- F against l for a wire in a field.
- Projectile range against u² → g (with the launch angle fixed at 45°).

**Parts menu** (choose 5–6, totalling 10):
- variables (2); a hypothesis (1); why this is plotted (1);
- a missing table value (1–2); plot with a line of best fit on axes provided (3); gradient with unit (2);
- a constant from the gradient (2); a prediction from the line (2);
- random or systematic error, with its direction (1–2); an improvement (1).

**Section A staples:**
- accuracy versus precision; repeating to reduce random error; timing 10 oscillations;
- uncertainty of a ruler reading; handling an outlier.
